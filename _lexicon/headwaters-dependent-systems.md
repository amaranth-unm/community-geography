# One or two sentences: shown on the word's card and at the top of its page.

  - text: >-
      In HDS, the management of water as a resource for both environment and
      human use is explored through a lens of socio-hydrology, where the
      physical systems of water are understood both in terms of their natural
      systems, and humans' role within them. The Indigenous conception of
      “Water is Life” is held in equity with western science conceptions of
      ecosystem services and resilience. In HDS, it is assumed that as soon as
      humans start to control water, inequalities are created.
    credit: "Credit: Alex Fremier, Washington State University"
  - heading: HDS emphasizes
    text: |-
      1. the **role of recharge and storage** in supporting the volume, timing, and **quality of water supply available to downstream** ecosystems and people
      2. **the coupled interactions between the biophysical, human and ecological components of a system** that require an understanding of how humans govern the system, such as how institutions, politics, power, and equity
      3. **the additional pressures that out-of-basin water demands create** (e.g., energy and food production)
      4. the view that **stationary approaches to management are incompatible** given climate change and need to be replaced with dynamic approaches (e.g., resilience)
      5. the view that humans and the environment are inseparable, so **water is life and not simply a commodity**
    credit: "Credit: Alex Fremier, Washington State University"
