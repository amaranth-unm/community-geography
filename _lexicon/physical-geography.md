      The field of physical geography has evolved over time. Where in the 19th
      century geographers were primarily concerned with cartography (mapping
      space to understand the natural world), today, due to the Anthropocene,
      human impact on natural systems cannot be ignored. Humans have so vastly
      altered our landscapes that we are impacting the physical systems of the
      planet. From *heat islands* in dense cities, *soil erosion* from
      agriculture, and *rapid aridity* in watersheds due to human-induced
      climate change, physical geography is inextricably linked to human
      presence on the earth. Spatial mapping tools like *GIS* and *remote
      sensing* offer opportunities to evolve our understanding of the physical
      world like never before. These fast-evolving cartographic tools bring
      human and natural interactions closer than ever, and can be tools in
      climate mitigation and adaptation efforts.
