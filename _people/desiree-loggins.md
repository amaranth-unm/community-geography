Desiree is a second year Ph.D. student in the Department of Geography and Environmental Studies at UNM. Her research centers place-based communities and their relationships to land, water, and modern environmental management in New Mexico. Her dissertation will focus on the political ecology of gender and technology in acequia governance and acequia lifeways. Prior to academia, Desiree worked at the National Audubon Society as a facilitator, organizer, and advocate for flowing rivers, working lands, and resilient communities in the arid West. She has an undergraduate degree in Environmental Studies from University of California, Santa Cruz, and a Masters of Science in Geography and Environmental Studies. Read her latest essay on the afterlife of colonial water infrastructure in the anthology, [Water Bodies: Love Letters to the Most  Abundant Substance on Earth.](https://www.torreyhouse.org/water-bodies) Desiree is also collaborating with [CESOS](https://www.cesoss.org/) on community engaged work as a Center fellow.
